Hot Rivets in a Calorimeter

Hot copper rivets → water in a copper calorimeter

Rivets at 100 °C are dropped into water at 15 °C. Energy flows from hot to cold until everything reaches the same temperature. Each moving dot is a fixed packet of energy.

c(water) = 4180 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, c(copper) = 390 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹. Assumes no energy is lost to the surroundings (lagging and lid) and the calorimeter stays at the water's temperature.

Temperature against time

The curves are steepest at the start, when the temperature difference is largest. As the gap closes, energy flows more slowly, and the dots thin out.

What the energy depends on

Q = m c Δθ

m, c and Δθ are all above the line, so as each one gets bigger, Q gets bigger: Q ∝ m, Q ∝ c, Q ∝ Δθ.

Rearranged, the equation defines c:

c = Q / (m Δθ)

Specific heat capacity is the energy per unit mass per unit temperature change. Its units fall out of the equation: J / (kg × K) = J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.

Water's c is more than ten times copper's, which is why the water takes almost all the energy even though the calorimeter warms by the same amount.
