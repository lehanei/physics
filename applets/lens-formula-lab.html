The lens formula for a converging lens

Aim: to verify the mathematical model 1/f = 1/u + 1/v for a converging lens, and to find its focal length.

Part A · The physics

What the equation tells you

WHAT?

What does the image distance depend on?

1/v = 1/f − 1/u

Two things: the object distance u and the focal length f of the lens.

HOW?

How does it depend on them?

  • As u gets bigger, 1/u gets smaller, so 1/v gets bigger and v gets smaller. Move the object away and the image moves in towards f.
  • As u gets close to f, 1/v gets close to zero and v gets very large.
  • 1/v is not proportional to 1/u: it falls by the same amount as 1/u rises. That is a straight line of slope −1.
HOW?

How do we show it in the lab?

Measure v for a range of u values. Plot 1/v against 1/u: a straight line of slope −1 that cuts both axes at 1/f.

Or rearrange to uv = f(u + v) and plot uv against u + v: a straight line through the origin with slope f.

The equation as a definition, and its units

Focal length f: the distance from the centre of the lens to the principal focus, where rays parallel to the axis meet after passing through the lens.

1/f is the power of the lens: P = 1/f. The power of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length.

Units: 1 over the units of f = 1/m = m⁻¹, called the dioptre (D). Every term in the equation, 1/f, 1/u and 1/v, has units of m⁻¹, so the equation balances.

Before you start

u is the distance from the object to the centre of the lens. v is the distance from the centre of the lens to the image.

Real is positive: real distances are positive. If u is less than f, the formula gives a negative v: the image is virtual and cannot be caught on a screen.

This is why you first find f roughly, so that every u you choose is bigger than f.

Part D · Check your understanding

Questions on this experiment

The usual questions

Q1How is an approximate value of the focal length found?

Focus the image of a distant object, such as a window or a tree outside, on a screen. Measure the distance from the lens to the screen. Rays from a distant object are almost parallel, so they meet at the focus.

Q2Why find the approximate focal length first?

So that every object distance used is bigger than f. If u is less than f, the image is virtual and cannot be formed on a screen.

Q3How is the position of the image found?

Move the screen back and forth until a sharp image of the cross-wire appears. Approach the sharp position from both sides and take the middle.

Q4Why are u and v measured to the centre of the lens?

The formula uses distances from the optical centre of the lens. Measuring to the edge of the lens or its holder adds a systematic error.

Q5Describe the image formed on the screen.

Real and inverted. It is magnified when u is between f and 2f, the same size at u = 2f, and diminished beyond 2f.

Q6Why is it hard to find the image when u is only slightly bigger than f?

v becomes very large, so the image is far away, large and dim. It stays roughly in focus over a long distance, so its exact position is hard to judge.

Q7How does the graph of 1/v against 1/u verify the formula?

It is a straight line with slope −1. Rearranged, the formula is 1/v = (−1)(1/u) + 1/f, which has the form y = mx + c with m = −1 and c = 1/f. Both intercepts equal 1/f.

Q8Why does the graph of uv against u + v go through the origin?

Multiply 1/f = 1/u + 1/v by fuv: uv = f(u + v). So uv is directly proportional to u + v, and the slope is f.

Q9Give two precautions.

Keep the centres of the object, lens and screen at the same height on one straight line. Work in a dimmed room so the image is clear. Read the metre stick at eye level to avoid parallax. Use a range of u values.

Q10A lens has a focal length of 20 cm. What is its power?

P = 1/f = 1/0.20 m = 5 m⁻¹ (5 dioptres). f must be in metres.

Percentage error

P1The screen position can be judged to ±0.5 cm. Calculate the percentage error in v = 20.0 cm and in v = 60.0 cm.

(0.5/20.0) × 100 = 2.5%. (0.5/60.0) × 100 = 0.8%.

But in practice the sharp position is much harder to judge when v is large, so the real uncertainty grows with v.

P2Why should you avoid values of u very close to f?

v becomes very large and the image is blurred over a long range of screen positions. The error in v becomes large.

P3Why is the value of f from a graph more reliable than from one pair of readings?

The line of best fit uses all the readings, so random errors in individual readings tend to cancel. It also shows up any reading that does not fit the pattern.
