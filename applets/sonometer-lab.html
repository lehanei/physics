Question 1(40)

A student investigated how the fundamental frequency f of a stretched string varied with its length l. The tension in the string was kept constant at 49 N. A set of tuning forks was used, and for each fork the length was adjusted until resonance occurred. The following data were recorded.

f (Hz)256288320384426.7512
l (cm)43.338.534.528.726.021.7

(i) Draw a labelled diagram of the apparatus.

(ii) How did the student know that resonance had occurred?

(iii) How was the tension kept constant?

(iv) Draw a suitable graph to show the relationship between f and l.

(v) What does your graph show?

(vi) Use your graph to calculate the mass per unit length of the string.

Answers

(i) A sonometer with a wire over two bridges (one movable), a paper rider at the midpoint, a tuning fork on the box, and the wire passing over a pulley to a mass hanger. Label the length l between the bridges.

(ii) The paper rider at the midpoint of the wire jumped off.

(iii) The same mass was left hanging on the wire throughout.

(iv) 1/l values (m⁻¹): 2.31, 2.60, 2.90, 3.48, 3.85, 4.61. Plot f on the y-axis against 1/l on the x-axis. The points lie on a straight line through the origin.

(v) f is inversely proportional to l.

(vi) Slope ≈ 111 Hz m. Slope = ½√(T/μ), so μ = T/(4 × slope²) = 49/(4 × 111²) ≈ 1.0 × 10⁻³ kg m⁻¹.

Question 2(40)

A student investigated how the fundamental frequency f of a stretched string varied with its tension T. The length of the string was kept at 40 cm. The string was set vibrating using a signal generator and a magnet. The following data were recorded.

T (N)102030405060
f (Hz)140198241280313342

(i) Describe how the string was set vibrating and how the fundamental frequency was found.

(ii) Why was the magnet placed at the midpoint of the string?

(iii) Draw a suitable graph to show the relationship between f and T.

(iv) Use your graph to calculate the mass per unit length of the string.

(v) With T = 10 N, the student also found the string vibrating strongly, in two loops, at 280 Hz. Explain this.

Answers

(i) An alternating current from the signal generator passes through the wire, which sits between the poles of the magnet. The force on the current-carrying wire makes it vibrate at the generator's frequency. The frequency is increased from a low value until the paper rider jumps off. The lowest such frequency is the fundamental.

(ii) The midpoint is an antinode of the fundamental, so the driving force there produces the largest vibration.

(iii) √T values (N½): 3.16, 4.47, 5.48, 6.32, 7.07, 7.75. Plot f against √T. The points lie on a straight line through the origin, so f ∝ √T.

(iv) Slope ≈ 44.2 Hz N⁻½. Slope = 1/(2l√μ), so μ = 1/(4l² × slope²) = 1/(4 × 0.40² × 44.2²) ≈ 8.0 × 10⁻⁴ kg m⁻¹.

(v) 280 Hz = 2 × 140 Hz, so this is the 2nd harmonic. The string vibrates in two loops, with a node at the midpoint.

Question 3(56)

(a) What is meant by resonance?

(b) Explain how a stationary wave is formed on a stretched string.

(c) What is a node?

(d) A guitar string of length 60 cm vibrates with a fundamental frequency of 250 Hz. Calculate (i) the wavelength of the fundamental, (ii) the speed of the wave on the string, (iii) the frequency of the 3rd harmonic.

(e) The tension in the string is 90 N. Calculate the mass per unit length of the string.

(f) The tension is increased to 360 N. What is the new fundamental frequency? Explain your answer.

Answers

(a) Resonance occurs when the driving frequency equals the natural frequency of a body, so energy is transferred efficiently and the amplitude becomes large.

(b) A wave travels along the string and reflects at the fixed ends. The incident and reflected waves, with the same frequency and amplitude, interfere to form a stationary wave.

(c) A point on a stationary wave that does not move: the amplitude there is always zero.

(d) (i) l = λ/2, so λ = 2 × 0.60 = 1.2 m. (ii) v = fλ = 250 × 1.2 = 300 m s⁻¹. (iii) f3 = 3 × 250 = 750 Hz.

(e) f = (1/2l)√(T/μ) and v = 2lf, so v = √(T/μ) and μ = T/v² = 90/300² = 1.0 × 10⁻³ kg m⁻¹.

(f) The tension is multiplied by 4, and f ∝ √T, so f is multiplied by √4 = 2. The new frequency is 500 Hz.
