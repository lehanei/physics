Before you start

You need two rays to find where the image of a point is. The image is where the rays meet.

LensRay 1Ray 2
ConvergingIn parallel → out through the focus“My favourite ray”: straight through the centre of the lens
DivergingIn parallel → out as if from the focus“My favourite ray”: straight through the centre of the lens

If the rays really meet, the image is real and can be caught on a screen. If they only appear to come from a point (draw these lines dashed), the image is virtual.

Virtual means negative: a virtual image has a negative v, and a diverging lens has a negative f.

Reading 1/f = 1/u + 1/v: for a fixed lens, f does not change. So as u gets smaller, 1/u gets bigger, 1/v must get smaller and v gets bigger. Move the object in towards the lens and the image moves out away from it.

Look for: does the image ever change from virtual to real? Where is the image always found, compared with F? Explain from the equation why v is always negative: f is negative, so 1/f − 1/u is negative wherever the object is.

Compound lenses

The image formed by lens 1 (I₁) becomes the object for lens 2.

  • Microscope: where is the object compared with F₁? Is I₁ real or virtual, magnified or diminished? Where is I₁ compared with F₂?
  • Telescope: the light from a distant object arrives parallel. Where does the objective form I₁? Why is the eyepiece placed so that I₁ is just inside F₂?

In both, the eyepiece acts as a magnifying glass on I₁. Compare this with position 6 on the converging tab.

The eye

  1. Normal eye: move the object from very far to 25 cm. Watch the lens. Does it get thicker or thinner as the object comes closer? Move it to 15 cm. What happens on the retina, and why?
  2. Short-sighted eye: find the distances where the image is blurred. Is the light focusing in front of or behind the retina? Try each type of spectacle lens and find the one that gives a sharp image of a very distant object.
  3. Long-sighted eye: look at 25 cm. Where is the light focusing? Find the spectacle lens that fixes it. Then keep the spectacles on and look at something very far away. What happens?

Explain in one sentence each: why a short-sighted eye needs a diverging lens, and why a long-sighted eye needs a converging lens.

Exam questions

Leaving Certificate Higher Level style. Answer in your copy first, then open the marking scheme. Mark allocations are a guide only.

Question 1: Converging lens

An object is placed 20 cm from a converging lens of focal length 15 cm.

  1. Draw a ray diagram to show how the image is formed. (9)
  2. Calculate the position of the image. (9)
  3. Give two characteristics of the image. (6)

The object is now moved to 10 cm from the lens.

  1. Calculate the new position of the image. (9)
  2. What does the sign of your answer tell you about the image? (6)
Show marking scheme
  1. Ray parallel to the axis, refracted through F (3). Ray through the centre of the lens, undeviated (3). Image shown where the rays meet, inverted, beyond 2F (3).
  2. 1/15 = 1/20 + 1/v (3) → 1/v = 1/60 (3) → v = 60 cm on the opposite side of the lens (3).
  3. Any two (3 + 3): real, inverted, magnified.
  4. 1/15 = 1/10 + 1/v (3) → 1/v = −1/30 (3) → v = −30 cm (3).
  5. Negative, so the image is virtual (3); on the same side of the lens as the object (3). Also accept: upright, magnified.

Question 2: Diverging lens

An object is placed 30 cm from a diverging lens of focal length 20 cm.

  1. Draw a ray diagram to show how the image is formed. (9)
  2. Calculate the position of the image. (9)
  3. Use the lens formula to explain why a diverging lens can never form a real image of a real object. (6)
Show marking scheme
  1. Ray parallel to the axis, refracted out as if from F, with the dashed line back to F (3). Ray through the centre of the lens, undeviated (3). Upright image between F and the lens, drawn dashed (3).
  2. f = −20 cm (3). 1/v = −1/20 − 1/30 = −1/12 (3) → v = −12 cm: virtual, 12 cm from the lens (3).
  3. 1/v = 1/f − 1/u. f is negative and u is positive, so both terms are negative (3). So 1/v is always negative and v is always negative: the image is always virtual (3).

Question 3: Experiment

In an experiment to measure the focal length of a converging lens, a student recorded these values of the object distance u and the image distance v.

u (cm)20.025.030.040.050.0
v (cm)60.237.430.123.921.5
  1. Describe how the image distance was found. (6)
  2. Use all the data to calculate the focal length of the lens. (12)
  3. Explain why a graph of 1/v against 1/u is a straight line, and how f can be found from it. (6)
  4. Why can this method not be used directly to find the focal length of a diverging lens? (6)
Show marking scheme
  1. Move a screen until a sharp image of the object (e.g. a lamp-box crosshair) forms on it (3). Measure the distance from the lens to the screen with a metre stick (3).
  2. Use 1/f = 1/u + 1/v for each pair (3): f = 15.01, 14.98, 15.02, 14.96, 15.03 cm (6). Average f = 15.0 cm (3).
  3. 1/v = −1/u + 1/f has the form y = mx + c, so the graph is a straight line of slope −1 (3). Each intercept equals 1/f ≈ 0.067 cm⁻¹, so f ≈ 15 cm (3).
  4. A diverging lens forms a virtual image (3), which cannot be caught on a screen, so v cannot be measured (3).

Question 4: The eye

  1. Explain how the eye focuses on objects at different distances. (6)
  2. A person’s near point is 60 cm. Name this defect and explain why they cannot read a book held 25 cm away. (6)
  3. Calculate the focal length of the spectacle lens that lets them read at 25 cm. State the type of lens. Ignore the distance between the lens and the eye. (9)
  4. Another person’s far point is 2 m. Calculate the focal length of the lens needed to correct their vision, and state the type of lens. (9)
Show marking scheme
  1. The ciliary muscles change the shape of the lens, which changes its focal length (3). Near object: the lens becomes more rounded, with a shorter focal length; far object: thinner, with a longer focal length. This is accommodation (3).
  2. Long sight (3). The lens cannot become rounded enough, so light from 25 cm would focus behind the retina and the image is blurred (3).
  3. The lens must form a virtual image of the book at 60 cm, so v = −60 cm (3). 1/f = 1/25 + 1/(−60) = 7/300 (3) → f = +42.9 cm, converging (3).
  4. Short sight. A distant object (u → ∞) must form a virtual image at 200 cm, so v = −200 cm (3). 1/f = 0 + 1/(−200) (3) → f = −200 cm, diverging (3).

Question 5: Compound lenses

A compound microscope has an objective of focal length 5 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 10 cm, placed 25 cm apart. An object is placed 7 cm from the objective.

  1. Calculate the position of the image formed by the objective. (6)
  2. Calculate the position of the final image. (9)
  3. Explain why the image formed by the objective must lie just inside the focus of the eyepiece. (6)

An astronomical telescope uses an objective of focal length 40 cm.

  1. Where does the objective form the image of a distant star? Explain. (6)
Show marking scheme
  1. 1/5 = 1/7 + 1/v₁ (3) → v₁ = 17.5 cm from the objective (3).
  2. u₂ = 25 − 17.5 = 7.5 cm (3). 1/10 = 1/7.5 + 1/v₂ → 1/v₂ = −1/30 (3) → v₂ = −30 cm: a virtual image 30 cm from the eyepiece, on the objective side (3).
  3. The eyepiece then acts as a magnifying glass (3), forming a large virtual image of I₁ (3).
  4. At the focal point of the objective, 40 cm from it (3). Light from a distant star arrives parallel, and parallel rays converge to the focus (3).
